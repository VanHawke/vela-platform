Why each option, in plain English

Option 1 — Warm Charcoal: Safest move. Looks like Linear/Notion/Vercel dark mode. Single purple accent removes the purple-vs-teal tension. Slight warmth (#14141a) makes the screen less fatiguing during long sessions.

Option 2 — Slate & Ember: Distinctive. The orange accent picks up motorsport heritage (Haas red-orange, McLaren papaya) without being cartoony. Riskier — orange can feel cheap if used wrong, but at low saturation as an accent it works.

Option 3 — Deep Blue: Most B2B-trustworthy. If Kiko is going to be sold to enterprise sponsorship buyers, blue reads as serious and credible. Loses some uniqueness — every B2B SaaS uses this.

Option 4 — Espresso & Ivory (my pick): The only one that aligns with Van Hawke's actual brand identity. You're not building a generic SaaS — you're building a tool for cultural-performance positioning. Champagne gold on warm espresso reads like a luxury fashion editorial in dark mode. None of your competitors look like this. It's the one that says "this was built by someone who cares about taste" before the user has read a single sentence.

Note on contrast: All four options pass WCAG AA for body text (4.5:1) and AAA for headings (7:1+). Your current palette fails AA on the secondary text (rgba(238,238,238,0.45) = 4.1:1) and fails badly on tertiary text at 0.30 opacity. That's why "it's difficult to read at times" — it literally is, by accessibility standards.
